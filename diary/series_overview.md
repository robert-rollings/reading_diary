# Other books or series of books I have read and enjoyed

Sorted by `Rating` and alphabetically by `Author`.

| Book Series | Author | Rating | Review |
|--------------|---------|---------|---------|
| **The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy** | Douglas Adams | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | A childhood favorite. Cried with laughter. Re-read so much as a child that my anthology fell apart. |
| **The Expanse** | James S. A. Corey | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | Not great literature but so entertaining. Like watching a movie. I became caught up with the world and the characters. I enjoyed the "realistic" portrayal of a future where humanities problems haven't been solved but rather expanded in scope. The optimistic end turned these novels from a bleak prediction to one of ultimate hope for humanity. |
| **The Wheel of Time** | Robert Jordan | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | The series I have loved the most. The epic scape, intricate world building and action. I agree with critics who say that the middle volumes are overly long and drag. However it inspired my imagination, brought me to tears and shouts of triumph. I grew to know, love and hate the characters. I have re-read the full series three times. |
| **The Three Body Problem** | Liu Cixin | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | One of my favorite book series. I loved the concept of a deadline to save the earth and how it would impact society over centuries. The interplay between the cultural revolution and the modern day was told brilliantly. Strange and compelling. |
| **The Wolf Hall Trilogy** | Hilary Mantel | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | Brought the world of the court and times of Henry VIII to life. I loved the characters and the world that HM built. The characters danced in my mind and their voices sang in my head. |
| **The Culture Series** | Iain Banks | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | Loved the world that was built and the optimism for the future. One could argue that these novels are techno-humanist in their message. Though they are more adventure stories. "The Player of Games" is by far my favorite novel. Exploring sexual politics, equality and morality. I like the fact that moral equivalence is rejected. |
| **The Song of Ice and Fire** | George R. R. Martin | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | A classic, third favorite fantasy series so far (after Wheel of Time and Malazan Book of the Fallen). |
| **The Lacuna** | Barbara Kingsolver | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | A beautiful description of a lost time in Mexico and the life of a young man. Weaving in Mexican and World history and betrayal. |
| **Moby Dick** | Herman Melville | ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️ | I read this when I was about 16 on a summer holiday. While I can't remember much of the novel I know I enjoyed it a great deal. I'm sure I will re-read at some point. |
| **Children of Time** | Adrian Tchaikovsky | ⭐️⭐️⭐️ | A great take on science fiction and the lost earth genre. The spiders were a great way to explore what it means to be a moral creature and to explore the value of life. |
| **The Handmaid’s Tale** | Margaret Atwood | ⭐️⭐️⭐️ | Difficult to read due to its content but fascinating and alarming. |
| **The Witcher** | Andrzej Sapkowski | ⭐️⭐️⭐️ | Enjoyable short stories. There is some exploration of themes of love, loss, betrayal and compassion. Not great literature. Good beach/holiday reading. |
| **Let the Right One In** | John Ajvide Lindqvist | ⭐️⭐️⭐️ | Read in my early 20s, loved the dark moodiness of the story. One of the first if not the first horror novel I have read. |
| **American Gods** | Neil Gaiman | ⭐️⭐️ | A modern fairytale that I enjoyed. The world building and characterisation was engaging. However I have found that Neil Gaiman's characters are very similar in each novel and his writing of female characters is one dimensional. |
| **The MaddAddam Trilogy** | Margaret Atwood | ⭐️⭐️ | A great satirical post-apocalyptic series. |
| **Dune** | Frank Herbert | ⭐️⭐️ | Highly variable. I enjoyed the first three and last two novels but found the writing style overly stylized. There is so much love for Dune that I felt I needed to read it as a Sci-Fi and fantasy fan. |
