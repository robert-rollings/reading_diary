## The reality dysfunction by Peter F. Hamilton

Hamilton writes big. His ideas, the worlds and the people and personalities he creates are of a giant scale and the world he creates in _The Reality Dysfunction_ are all the things one would expect of Hamilton. The fusion of horror and the supernatural possession of the living by the dead was not expected. He just about manages to bring this together within a science fiction plot without stretching credulity too far.

Joshua, Horst, Quinn, Ione and Syrinx are all drawn beautifully, their motivations, sense of emotion clear. The habitats and ships, even the inert Lady Mac had personalities that you could believe and feel.

However, for me the pacing wasn't quite right. There were large parts of the story on Lalonde which stretched too long for me. Once Quinn and Laton had left I felt it hard to stay engaged and interested in what was happening there. We weren't shown enough of Horst and the children to really get to know them, apart from Jay, there was so much that could have been done here.

I will certainly continue to read the series and look forward to a climatic confrontation between Joshua and Quinn - for surely it has to come.
