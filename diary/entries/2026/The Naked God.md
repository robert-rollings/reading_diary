# The Naked God by Peter F. Hamilton

The Naked God was a corker. A really unputdownable book. So far the [[Nights Dawn]] series has been a good read, interesting conceit of the Paranormal having real physical basis and humans having to come to terms with their own immortality and the threat that the beyond has on those still alive in this realm. What sets the [[The Naked God]] apart is the pacing.

The chapters were long but broken into a series of scenes which introduced little cliff-hangers where high action scenes were left unresolved or emotional tension left unbroken. This made The Naked God very hard to put down. I was reading to 2am some nights with only an act of will making me put the novel down to get some sleep.

The resolution of the story arch across the three novels ([[The Reality Dysfunction]], [[The Neutronium Alchemist]] and [[The Naked God]]) relied on a deus ex machina of epic proportions. The "Silent God". I can find the use of this device, when it is sudden and unexpected, frustrating. Suggesting that the author had a failure of imagination. In Hamilton's case he set the "Silent God" up from the first novel. This made this series a quest to find this enigmatic entity.

Highly enjoyable. Another rich would with interesting concepts from [[Peter F. Hamilton]].
