The Dungeon crawler carl book I enjoyed the most. On reflection what Dinniman started as a joke with the first novel _[[Dungeon Crawler Carl]]_ has turned into an epic tale. Where I have been frustrated by Neil Gaiman's re-use of plot structures and tropes I find Dinniman's Dungeon Crawler carl compelling.

The differences between Gaiman's "young-man discovers mystical or magic history and destiny, moves through world, achieves self-realisation and happiness" story arc feels unfulfilling for me. However, each [[Dungeon Crawler Carl (series)|Dungeon Crawler Carl]] novel is, by design, the same structure. This could have boxed Dinniman in, however it is the inner life and emotional realism of the characters, in an absurd world, that makes these novels so readable. They feel grief and you feel it with them, the feel excitement and joy. Most importantly the characters are realised with enough depth that you start to love them too. I can predict what Doughnut will do. Not because she is predictable but _because I know her so well_. To write a character where you feel they are a friend is a real skill and a gift to readers.

*In the Eye of the Bedlam Bride* Dinniman has really got into his stride. His parody of Capitalism and the hypocrisy of those who enjoy violence so long as they themselves aren't victims is brought to the fore. This novels could easily be set in ancient Rome and had a gladiator as protagonist.

Dinniman doesn't aim for seriousness. His parody and political message are loosely held. His primary goal is entertainment. He has lifted this from comic horror to a battle of wills between Carl, Doughnut, the AI and the polity of the wider galaxy. The violence is slapstick but the tragedy real. For example Carl when possessed plucks out a friends eye. He feels guilty, he is torn. But the moment itself was darkly funny - I don't have the literary skills to explain why, but this novel is full of those moments. The scenarios that Dinniman imagines are elaborate (zombie turkeys infecting the worlds oceans with zombie sea creatures), darkly funny and full of love for his characters.

Dinniman says, in his authors note, that these books will be forgotten as pieces of pop culture often are. I don't think Dinniman is correct. These books tap into an anger of the modern capitalist age. We all feel a little like Carl. Forced to live in a world that treats us as expendable, where the powerful ignore us and use us for our their own ends. The purchasing of politics by the rich and the extraction of wealth into a smaller percentage of society may make people see these novels as an insightful commentary on a point in history but with a universal message - that love and friendship are ultimately the only thing that matters. I am grateful to him for writing these delightful books. What makes this message so powerful is that it is wrapped in characters people can love and root for.
