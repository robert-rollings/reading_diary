An interesting exploration of free will and the nature of existence. What is it like to inhabit more than one body at once, how can one be in conflict with oneself but at the same time whole and a single unitary person.

The influence of Ancillary Justice on [[Martha Wells]] and [[Christopher Ruocchio]]
are clear. The human but not human, the sentient, but morally complex and enslaved, enforcer of security [[Martha Wells| Well's Murder Bot]] and [[Christopher Ruocchio|Rucchio's SOMs]] are clearly influenced by [[Martha Wells|Wells]]. One could also argue that the imperial religion of the Solan Empire and its society were influenced, not just by ancient Rome but also by the Radch.

However, I did not feel for Breq (formerly _Justice of Toren_ One Esk 19), her vulnerability did not feel real to me, her empathy cold and not real. I found myself empathising with Anaander Mianaai (the Tyrant of the Radch) far more than One Esk.

Enjoyable but unsatisfying. No desire to read further in the series.
