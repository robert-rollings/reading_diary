# Fool's Errand by Robin Hobb

I finished the Fools Errand thinking two things. First, that I had enjoyed the novel and was glad to return again to Fitz, the Fool and The Seven Duchies and also that I don't know where Hobb is going and what her message is. With other writers with a similar sized ambition (Robert Jordan, Steven Erikson) there is a moral, message or philsophy that reaches through their work and binds it together. I'm yet to puzzle through what this is for Robin Hobb. 

There are questions of class (Wit vs Skill), loyalty, love and the natural world and our (human) part in it. But I can't see a central theme or lesson.

There are two questions therefore that this leaves me. First, does it matter, and second am I just too dense to see it?
